# Category Language

Layer 19 names the product category so the public surface reads as
Opportunity Geography Intelligence and Career Market Intelligence, not as a
job board.

## Primary Category Terms

### Opportunity geography

Opportunity geography describes how career opportunity varies by region,
employer presence, sector mix, role access, evidence quality, and time. It is
the geographic view of where opportunity appears concentrated, accessible,
durable, emerging, or under-covered.

Use this term when explaining regional comparison, market maps, geographic
tradeoffs, company-region presence, and opportunity concentration.

### Career market intelligence

Career market intelligence is structured analysis of labor-market conditions
for career planning. It combines regional opportunity density, employer
presence, hiring signals, early-career access, source freshness, confidence,
coverage, and limitations.

Use this term when describing the product category, public report posture,
methodology, and decision context. It should not imply exhaustive live job
listings, guaranteed outcomes, or individualized career advice.

### Company-region presence

Company-region presence means evidence that a company has meaningful activity
in a specific geography. Evidence may include offices, hiring traces, role
concentration, internship signals, partnerships, source observations, or other
verified market context.

Presence is not the same as an open job. Weak, stale, or missing presence
evidence must be labeled honestly.

### Early-career access

Early-career access describes whether a market, employer, or role family has
visible pathways for students, interns, new graduates, apprentices, career
switchers, and candidates without extensive prior experience.

It can include internships, entry-level roles, university recruiting patterns,
training pathways, accessible role families, and supporting ecosystem signals.

### Market fit

Market fit is the directional alignment between a person's career goals and
the observed market evidence for regions, companies, industries, role families,
career stage, preferences, and constraints.

Market fit is explanatory context. It is not a guarantee, placement decision,
financial recommendation, legal advice, or individualized career advice.

### Regional opportunity density

Regional opportunity density describes the concentration of relevant employers,
hiring signals, company-region presence, sector activity, access pathways, and
evidence quality in a geography.

Density should be read with coverage and freshness context. A low-density or
unknown result may reflect missing data, source limitations, or incomplete
coverage rather than true absence of opportunity.

### Career-market signal

A career-market signal is structured evidence or a derived indicator about
opportunity conditions. Signals can describe presence, hiring, entry-level
access, sector concentration, freshness, coverage, confidence, momentum, or
limitations.

Signals may be strong, weak, stale, partial, missing, or inferred. Public
language must preserve those qualifiers.

## Public Language Rules

- Describe the product as career market intelligence or opportunity geography
  intelligence.
- Explain visible outputs as evidence, signals, summaries, indicators, or
  directional intelligence.
- Keep source freshness, coverage limits, and confidence context near claims.
- Use "unknown", "not observed", or "insufficient evidence" when data is
  missing.
- Avoid implying the product is a live job board, exhaustive source of open
  jobs, application system, recruiter, school placement tool, or outcome
  guarantee.
- Avoid saying a market "has no opportunity" when the evidence is merely
  missing or weak.

## Category Boundary

Layer 19 language does not change canonical data, recommendation ranking,
market coverage, refresh authority, billing, institutional permissions,
personalization, report generation, or benchmark scoring. Batch 19A only
defines vocabulary and public explanation surfaces.
